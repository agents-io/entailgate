# Entailgate v0.2 roadmap: selective verification

Status: alpha foundations implemented; high-risk release gate not met
Date created: 2026-08-22
Last updated: 2026-08-23
Objective: keep byte-level audit identity while re-verifying only claims whose meaning or evidence binding may have changed.

## Product question

How can a revised answer or legal draft reuse trustworthy prior checks after harmless prose edits without allowing a material change to inherit a stale pass?

The answer is two different layers of identity:

1. **Document SHA-256 is an audit seal.** It identifies the exact artifact that was reviewed. Computing it is cheap and does not call an AI.
2. **Claim fingerprints control re-verification.** A changed document triggers extraction and comparison. Only added, materially changed, evidence-changed, or uncertain claims require semantic review.

Deleting whole-document hashes would weaken the evidence chain. Treating every changed hash as a reason to repeat every semantic check wastes resources. v0.2 keeps the first property and removes the second behaviour.

## Selective invalidation contract

For every revision:

1. hash the complete current artifact for provenance;
2. extract the material-claim inventory;
3. compute a stable material signature for each claim;
4. compare the new inventory with the previously verified inventory;
5. reuse an attestation only when the material signature, proof binding, evidence-content hash, jurisdiction, relevant date, and checker version are unchanged;
6. re-verify added or materially changed claims;
7. treat unmatched, ambiguous, or low-confidence comparisons as `UNCERTAIN`, never as reusable;
8. recompute complete coverage before allowing a final `PASS`.

Protected changes that always invalidate the affected claim include:

- quotation text or pinpoint;
- citation, source, authority tier, jurisdiction, or effective date;
- number, amount, date, deadline, identifier, actor, attribution, or negation;
- statute or policy section;
- legal test, exception, burden, procedural stage, requested finding, remedy, or reservation of rights;
- modal strength such as may/must/can/cannot and equivalent Chinese wording.

Connector, punctuation, formatting, and grammar edits may be reusable only after protected-token checks pass and the claim matcher is not uncertain. A pronoun edit is not automatically harmless because actors can be legally material.

## Evaluation design

Start with a deterministic protected-token/normalization baseline before MiniCheck, another NLI model, or an LLM judge.

Primary safety metrics:

- false-pass rate: contradicted or unsupported claims incorrectly allowed;
- false-reuse rate: materially changed claims incorrectly inheriting an old attestation;
- fabricated-citation survival rate;
- complete-coverage failure detection.

Secondary utility metrics:

- supported-claim recall;
- correct abstention rate and unnecessary-review rate;
- claim extraction precision/recall by claim type;
- latency, tokens, and cost per changed material claim rather than per document.

Report sample sizes and exact or bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Split by authority/document family with grouped partitions so passages from one decision cannot appear in both training and test. Maintain a temporal holdout for later statutes, policies, and decisions. Select thresholds on development data only.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harmless-edit classifier hides a real legal change | High | Critical false pass | Protected-token rules; `UNCERTAIN` fallback; false-reuse benchmark |
| Heading or pronoun context changes while sentence text stays the same | High | Stale attestation reuse | Fingerprint heading path and antecedent; claim scope requires `selfContained=true` |
| A genuine quote launders unsupported surrounding words | High | False exact-support pass | Exact proof is quote-only; wrapper propositions become separate claims |
| Claim extractor omits an assertion | High | False coverage | Independent coverage audit; extractor recall benchmark; never infer completeness from silence |
| Large rewrites make pairwise matching quadratic | Medium | Latency/resource exhaustion | Hard comparison budget with `UNCERTAIN` fallback now; indexed/bipartite matcher before release |
| OCR corrupts a quotation or section number | Medium | Wrong citation/quote | Per-page hashes, extraction method/version, quality remains `UNCHECKED`, visual review route |
| Benchmark leaks the same authority across splits | Medium | Inflated scores | Grouped split by authority/document family and temporal holdout |
| LLM judges share the generator's failure mode | High | Correlated false pass | Deterministic checks first; diverse calibrated signals; human gold labels |
| Public corpus licence is unclear | Medium | Blocks distribution | Store source/licence ledger; use originating-body or authorized channels |
| Cantonese labels are inconsistent | Medium | Unstable calibration | Written annotation guide, double annotation, adjudication, agreement reporting |

## Out of scope for v0.2

- claiming universal truth verification;
- silently deciding that a semantic rewrite is harmless;
- uploading private evidence to an external checker;
- using search snippets as authority text;
- executing sends, filings, bookings, or other actions;
- replacing an independent legal/source-integrity audit with a model score.

## Dogfood workflow status

`entailgate dogfood` exists so the first internal BC-legal run can happen on one machine without pretending anything is finished. What it enables is a **first-run internal dogfood**, nothing more:

- it is **not** a high-risk public release, and the v0.2 release gate below is unchanged and still unmet;
- it is **not** automatic legal verification: it extracts no claims, classifies no source authority, and reaches no legal conclusion;
- it does **not** perform selective revision reuse. Cross-version claim reuse remains the separate `claim-reuse-authorization` alpha gate and is not wired into this workflow;
- `ready` from ingestion still means technical extraction only. Every file parsing successfully is explicitly **not** treated as a complete evidence set.

A freshly initialised workspace is blocked by construction: coverage is incomplete, the claims list is empty, every source sits at authority tier `unknown`, and every human confirmation is `false`. Reaching the verifier requires a person to inventory the material claims, classify and scope the sources, and sign the checklist. A blocked run writes no trace, so a refusal never leaves an artifact that could later read as a verification record.

`manifest.json` is meant to be edited, so its content is deliberately not pinned to the hash it had at `init`. What is checked on every read is that it still belongs to this workspace (`requestId`, `snapshotId`, draft hash) and that everything in it the machine produced is still bound to what was ingested: source `sha256` (required, not merely consistent when present), source `title` and `sourceType`, chunk `sourceId`, `sha256`, text, and `locator`, and `subject.path`. Authority tier, jurisdictions, issuer, version, and dates stay human-editable, and nothing here can check whether the human got them right.

`chunk.structuredFacts` is rejected outright. Structured and derived proofs read that field as evidence, and this workflow's ingestion produces no structured facts, so there is nothing to bind one to. It stays refused until a separately validated structured-fact ingestion and binding contract exists.

A completed run reports `status: "CHECK_COMPLETED"`, never `VERIFIED`. The verdict is the `decision` field, which may be `ABSTAIN`, `HUMAN_REVIEW`, `REWRITE`, or `ASK`; exit zero still requires `PASS` with every action `ALLOW`.

Known limitations, stated rather than hidden:

- the workspace hashes are consistency seals against drift and stale files, not authenticated provenance; whoever can write the workspace can recompute them. Artifact names are therefore taken from the code's constants, not from the sealed metadata;
- the checklist records that a human asserted the review happened. It cannot demonstrate that the review was any good, it is not a hash-bound attestation, and it carries no identity — a checklist transplanted from another workspace with every confirmation already `true` reads as valid. This is an operator and authentication limitation, not something a recomputable hash can fix;
- nothing verifies that the authority tier a reviewer assigned to a source is correct. A blog post marked `primary` passes the gate. Automatic authority classification is out of scope, and this remains the largest place where the outcome depends on operator judgement;
- symlinks are refused at `init` and again at every reopen, but this is ordinary Node path traversal, not a hostile-filesystem sandbox. A concurrent local attacker can still swap a directory between the check and the open; `openat`-style directory handles are deliberately not a dependency here, so that race is **unresolved**;
- the gate requires every enumerated evidence entry to resolve, so one unreadable file in the directory blocks the run. That is deliberate, and it is the opposite of treating a whole directory as complete;
- the PDF/OCR sandbox caveats in workstream 3 are unchanged and apply to every file this workflow ingests;
- a partially written workspace is left on disk if `init` fails; nothing here deletes directory trees.

## Next implementation sequence

1. The verifier-policy gate that consumes a mapping is specified (spec section 6) and implemented in `src/reuse-policy.ts` as a separate `claim-reuse-authorization` artifact at schema `0.2.0`. It reads and replays the `revision-plan` trace itself, so nothing can authorize reuse from an unchecked in-memory mapping, and a trace that fails to read or replay yields no authorization at all. It decides one mapped attestation at a time — `REUSE_AUTHORIZED`, `REVERIFY_REQUIRED`, `POLICY_BLOCKED`, or `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED` — behind explicit deterministic gates: a `COMPLETE` mapping, an exact `MATCHED_REUSE_ITEM`/`REUSE` association to one uniquely identified current candidate, an attested claim text that really is the bound previous candidate's, a `SUPPORTED` verdict, an exact `(name, version, kind)` trust triple, a finite score over an explicit threshold, `checkedAt` neither future nor older than an explicit maximum age measured against a caller-supplied `asOf`, and an exact `snapshotId` **plus** `evidenceHash` pin. What it still cannot prove is jurisdiction, domain, and source currency: a `SemanticAttestation` carries none of them, so the gate pins `claimBindingHash` (which binds the v0.1 claim metadata) and requires the rest as explicit hash-bound caller declarations, recorded as declarations rather than verified. It never releases a document; incomplete automatic extraction stays an explicit `DOCUMENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED` condition even when a claim is reusable. The same closure holds on the read side: `evaluateClaimReuse`, `replayClaimReuseAuthorization`, and `authorizedClaimReuses` all take artifact paths through one closed, runtime-validated input and do their own reading and replaying, so no exported helper turns a caller-assembled replay, authorization, or decision list into an authorized reuse.

   The mapping underneath it is unchanged: `SemanticAttestation` carries an explicit draft-candidate binding (`src/draft-binding.ts`), plan items are mapped onto bound attestations by canonical hash equality (`src/prior-mapping.ts`), and the `revision-plan` trace at schema `0.2.1` stores the bound attestations, binds their canonical hash and the mapping into `artifactHash`, and recomputes both on replay. The binding was necessary because the fingerprints are different identity spaces: `SemanticAttestation.claimFingerprint` is computed over the isolated manifest claim text and binds an empty heading path, while an in-draft candidate fingerprint binds heading path, heading levels, semantic role, and rolling antecedent context. Measured in `tests/revision-trace.test.ts`, the two agree only for a sentence at the document root, and a sentence repeated twice yields two candidates sharing one fingerprint. A `MATCHED_REUSE_ITEM` entry and a `COMPLETE` status are still not reuse authorization; only the section 6 artifact is, and only for one claim at a time.
2. Resolve routed citations against official origins, build immutable authority cards,
   and assemble claim packets with deterministic context-expansion triggers.
3. Add a model-assisted extractor only as a second signal; disagreements with the deterministic inventory become `UNCERTAIN`.
4. Build the first public-authority annotation batch with evaluator-held test labels, dual annotation, and no private Evidence material.
5. Run deterministic, NLI, and diverse-judge baselines on train/dev; freeze thresholds before the first locked test.
6. Put untrusted PDF tooling behind a stronger OS/container sandbox and disk quota; keep PDF/OCR quality `UNCHECKED` until visual confirmation.
7. Forward-test one revised BC legal draft, then the Missed Call AI adapter, measuring source checks saved per changed material claim. The `entailgate dogfood` workspace is the first-run harness for the initial single-version pass; extending it to a revised draft requires the cross-version reuse work above and is deliberately out of scope for it today.

## Release gate

v0.2 cannot ship as a high-risk selective verifier until:

- every reused claim has a replayable mapping to its prior attestation, and a replayable authorization decision on top of it;
- protected material changes always force re-verification in the gold set;
- no fabricated citation survives the test suite;
- incomplete or uncertain extraction cannot produce `PASS`;
- benchmark results include sample sizes, confidence intervals, error classes, and frozen checker versions;
- a real revised legal draft demonstrates that harmless prose edits avoid semantic re-review while legal, factual, citation, date, and remedy changes do not.
